# Validation and evidence | What is and is not proven

**Document v2.0.0 | October 14, 2026 | Nathaniel Schroeder**

## Three evidence classes

1. **Synthetic engineering tests:** artificial records and expected results verifying the code and controls behave consistently.
2. **Source-backed market observations:** real public listings, bids or reported sold results whose provenance and limits are retained.
3. **Actual completed transactions:** matched purchase documents, real costs, sale proceeds, elapsed holding time and properly calculated realized margin.

These classes are **not interchangeable**.

## Things I refuse to count as profit

- The difference between a seller's ask and a projected listing price.
- A historical retail list minus auction purchase expense.
- A price shown at auction close without proof of hammer and mandatory costs.
- A model's low/base/high exit estimates.
- The return produced by a synthetic scenario.
- A public sold-price observation not linked to the exact asset and acquisition costs.

**Realized gross profit** requires an actual sold price less the complete relevant acquisition, reconditioning and transaction costs. A claim about **net profit** also requires correctly treating attributable operating, holding and other business expenses.

## What has to change before stronger claims

The next evidence package needs observed identifiers and configuration, a source chain from an actual purchase to an actual completed sale, settlement and expense support, and the ability to replay the original pre-decision estimates without rewriting history.

The system's most credible current characteristic is that it rejects or holds incomplete opportunities. The actual economic advantage of using it instead of current alternatives remains a pilot hypothesis.
