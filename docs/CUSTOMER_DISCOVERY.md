# Customer discovery and how it changed the hypothesis

**v2.0.0 | Nathaniel Schroeder**

## Research work

I completed a Great Plains regional I-Corps customer discovery cohort, held conversations with vehicle/equipment industry professionals and continued outreach to understand how buyers actually evaluate assets and where information is missing.

The founder-reported count is **31 dealer meetings**. Separately, the final course interview log documented **23 completed discovery conversations** at its recorded handoff. I am **not** treating those as identical samples, independent paid users, or proof of product-market fit.

## What changed in the research question

**Earlier hypothesis:** Buyers would benefit from fast vehicle/equipment valuation plus a clear protected offer amount.

**What industry conversations challenged:** Established dealers often already have pricing guides, auction data, internal sales histories and experienced appraisers. Providing another AI valuation answer alone is unlikely to justify a separate subscription.

**Stronger current hypothesis:** Value may exist in bringing scattered evidence, asset configuration, reconstruction/reconditioning costs, transportation, local resale context and missing approvals into one defensible *pre-acquisition decision record*.

A separate equipment-service-readiness track examined intake/parts/technician handoffs, but the current controlled roadmap prioritizes acquisition underwriting and outcome calibration. The service concept remains R&D, not a deployed service product.

## Evidence versus assumptions

| Observation | Interpretation | Open question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Multiple valuation and record sources already exist | A product must complement existing decision processes, not assume dealers lack technology | What information is still manually reconciled? |
| Condition, options and seasonality can materially change equipment economics | A single market price is insufficient | Can a repeatable proof checklist reduce avoidable mistakes? |
| Dealers use judgment and internal historical results | AI alone is a weak moat | What does AvLü do faster or more consistently? |
| Some participants described simpler information access as helpful | Potential usability signal | Would they return, pay, or change workflow? |

No individual participant endorsement or dealer partnership is claimed in this public summary.

## Next actual discovery experiment

Use a small set of anonymized historical opportunities that a willing participant is authorized to share. Capture the participant's original review time, sources and next decision *before* revealing any AvLü assessment. Measure differences in missing-proof discovery, duplicate work, decision traceability, total time and whether the participant uses it a second time.

Claims of demand should depend on repeated use, changed behavior and commercial willingness, not compliments during an interview.
