# Business model and commercialization hypotheses

**v2.2.0 | October 15, 2026 | Nathaniel Schroeder**

## The problem worth monetizing

Acquisition decisions often require reconciling multiple sources before an operator knows whether a unit merits inspection, negotiation or rejection. The initial commercial hypothesis is that small operators and dealers will pay for a workflow that materially reduces missed information and duplicated diligence.

## Customer hypotheses, not paying-customer claims

| Segment | Decision being improved | What I still need to learn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Independent mixed-inventory buyers | Whether an auction or private-source item merits attention | Frequency, pain, willingness to pay |
| Used-vehicle dealers | Whether a poorly documented acquisition survives proof and reconditioning review | Relative value versus existing appraisal tools |
| Equipment/attachment resellers | How transport, condition, configuration and exit timing alter the opportunity | Whether outcome data are obtainable and category-specific |
| Related software platforms | Whether a pre-acquisition review record is useful before inventory/build management | Integration cost and product overlap |

The project has **a working prototype and discovery activity** and now has an explicit **proposed pricing model**. It does **not** have proven price acceptance, contracted ARR, paying pilot conversion rates, measured churn/CAC, a signed source-data licensing agreement or independently verified savings.

## Product positioning

A narrow pre-acquisition **evidence and decision layer**. Not a replacement for every dealer-management system, market-price guide or human inspection.

The tested commercial hypotheses are a $199/month Independent tier ($1,990/year), $499/month Dealer Team, from $999/month for negotiated integration, and a $750–$1,500 one-time paid pilot. These are **not active rates accepted by paying customers**. The primary model is SaaS subscriptions; customer equipment resale proceeds, unsupported Parts Commerce revenue and separate service/inspection R&D are not existing ARR.

The 25-account model assumes $8,975 MRR, $2,430 in direct monthly costs and $9,000 in fixed monthly costs, meaning **a $2,455 monthly loss** before additional sales costs/tax. A $100,000 first-raise model is under evaluation and has not been funded.

## Proof that would justify charging

- People return with multiple real opportunities without founder prompting.
- A blind or paired comparison shows fewer overlooked issues and less manual searching.
- Users trust evidence lineage and understand why the system refuses weak claims.
- At least one economic buyer confirms budget ownership and a purchase process.
- Full servicing and data-access costs leave adequate margin after providing the product.

## Why I am seeking accelerator and mentor input

I want help narrowing a viable first segment, refining the go-to-market evidence, finding appropriately scoped pilots, and prioritizing authorized data access. A program introduction or application is **not** itself proof of acceptance or funding.

## Four questions for a potential pilot sponsor

1. What is your current cost of reviewing weak opportunities?
2. How many such reviews occur per week or month?
3. Which mistakes or evidence gaps meaningfully change a bid or repair decision?
4. What improvement would justify a paid trial, and who signs off?
