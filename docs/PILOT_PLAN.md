# Proposed controlled pilot | Acquisition review

**v2.4.0 | October 15, 2026 | Nathaniel Schroeder | Proposal, not an agreed pilot**

## Purpose

Determine whether AvLü meaningfully improves an operator's next-action decision using realistic acquisition opportunities. The pilot should measure a narrow workflow **without autonomous purchases**.

## Scope

- One consenting business or buyer group, one inventory category, and a limited batch of real but permissioned/anonymized records.
- Compare the participant's existing process with an AvLü-assisted review using information that would have been available at the decision date.
- No access to confidential systems, customer identities, seller accounts or private transactions unless an appropriate agreement authorizes it.
- No automatic seller contact, binding offers, payments or changes to the operator's systems.

## Paid pilot pricing under test

The proposed pilot fee is **$750–$1,500 one-time** for a fixed scope, a small comparison set and a measured outcome review. Five pilots at a hypothetical $1,000 fee yield $5,000 **one-time revenue**, not recurring ARR. The prospect-to-customer planning funnel targets **50 qualified accounts, 15 demos, five paid pilots and three subscriptions**. No pilot or conversion is reported as achieved.

## Four-stage experiment

| Stage | Action | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | Observe how the buyer reviews 5 to 10 real past opportunities | Timing, steps, tools, missed data, original decisions |
| Side-by-side | Run the same decision-time inputs through the bounded review workflow | Missing-proof flags, estimate assumptions, conflicts, false positives |
| Outcome review | Compare known final disposition records, only where authenticated and shareable | Avoided oversights and accuracy limits, not hindsight-inflated performance |
| Follow-up | Ask for independent second usage and a budget-holder discussion | Repeat behavior, improvement, next product decision |

## Economic impact and fairness

I will compare the current buyer's tools, an appropriately capable generic AI approach, and AvLü on the **same decision-time evidence**, counting all human research and extra review steps. The pilot must measure false holds and incremental administrative work, not just correctly flagged missing proof.

A fictional arithmetic example is available in the [investor tour](../investors.html#acquisition). My [customer ROI experiment](CUSTOMER_ROI_TEST.md) describes the actual measurement and what cannot be called cash savings.

## Pilot scorecard

- Median reviewer time and repeat manual lookups.
- Number of material missing-evidence items surfaced early.
- Number of false alarms and unnecessary HOLD outcomes.
- Agreement or disagreement with the participant's original decision.
- Estimated versus actual cost error where invoices exist.
- Repeat usage without founder assistance.
- Permission and source burden per screened item.
- Credible willingness to pay.

The scorecard must show **negative and inconclusive outcomes** rather than selecting only favorable cases.

## Stop or pivot conditions

If source licensing is unavailable, actual outcomes cannot be legally linked, the product increases work, or an incumbent already resolves the same workflow cheaply, I should narrow to a complement/integration or stop that wedge.

A real commercial pilot begins only with appropriate authorization and mutually understood evaluation boundaries.
