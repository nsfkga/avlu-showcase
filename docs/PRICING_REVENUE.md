# AvLü™ | Pricing and revenue architecture

**Proposed commercial research | v1.0.0 | October 15, 2026**  
**Owner: Nathaniel Schroeder**

## What the customer pays for

The core unit of value is not an AI prompt, a price lookup or a generated resale estimate. A customer would subscribe to a maintained decision workflow that assembles asset-specific evidence, exposes missing information, applies total-cost assumptions and retains review history.

The strongest initial target is the buyer who encounters a recurring stream of incomplete equipment, attachment or vehicle opportunities and needs to screen them before tying up capital.

## Proposed customer offers

| Tier | Price hypothesis | Primary use | Commercial qualification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Independent | **$199/month** | One buyer or small resale operator evaluating acquisitions | Starting pricing hypothesis, not validated |
| Annual Independent | **$1,990/year** | Same subscription with annual commitment | Equivalent to $165.83/month; approximately 16.7% below 12 monthly payments |
| Dealer Team | **$499/month** | Multi-user evidence review, owner records and team workflow | Future tier features and permissions need complete acceptance |
| Dealer Integration | **From $999/month** | Scoped data/API/system connection and service agreements | Negotiated after technical, legal and support costs are known |
| Paid pilot | **$750 to $1,500 one-time** | Limited evaluation with baseline, comparison and readout | Temporary service revenue, not recurring SaaS revenue |

**These offers are not publicly purchasable, guaranteed, approved contracts, verified demand or committed rate cards.** Included users, evaluation limits, source-data quotas, integration fees, support commitments and taxes must be specified before accepting payment.

## Why subscription first

A subscription supports repeat use across many inventory decisions and can scale without AvLü financing dealer inventory. Revenue should come from software access and maintained operations, not assuming AvLü owns the buyer's gross resale proceeds.

Additional possible future streams should remain separate from the base model:
- **One-time setup and implementation** for permissioned dealer integrations, where the time and deliverable are contracted.
- **Parts Commerce gross margin** only after supplier approval, wholesale pricing, fitment, fulfillment, returns and actual sellable SKUs are verified.
- **Digital Inspection coordination or premium workflow** only after consented operations, liability, providers and willingness to pay are established.
- **Equipment Service Readiness and Recovery** is separate historical R&D, not an active subscription revenue source.
- **Founder-owned equipment resale** would be a separate capital-at-risk trading activity, not evidence of SaaS recurring revenue.

Do not include these unlaunched streams in the core ARR scenario or treat customer equipment revenue as AvLü revenue.

## Example recurring-revenue math

For a purely illustrative 25-account scenario:
- 15 Independent accounts × $199 = **$2,985 monthly**.
- Eight Dealer Team accounts × $499 = **$3,992 monthly**.
- Two Dealer Integration accounts × $999 = **$1,998 monthly**.
- Total **$8,975 MRR**, or **$107,700 annualized ARR**.

A five-account Independent-only illustration is **$995 MRR**. A 100-account version of the same 60% / 32% / 8% segment proportions is **$35,900 MRR** or **$430,800 annualized ARR**.

**MRR and ARR are hypothetical rates, not cash collections, recognized annual revenue or forecasts.** Annual prepayments, discounts, pilots, cancellations and costs of service require separate treatment.

## How to test willingness to pay

Ask budget-holders to make a real choice after side-by-side evidence review:
1. Would they purchase a bounded paid pilot at the stated price?
2. Do they use the product again without founder facilitation?
3. Which tasks or mistakes actually matter enough to fund?
4. Is the product replacing cost, adding a necessary control or just creating another screen?
5. Which source rights, users, support and data freshness expectations are mandatory?

Measure conversion into **paid contracts** and customer retention, not enthusiasm or meeting counts. If buyers prefer a lower fixed review fee, a channel partnership or an incumbent add-on, the pricing hypothesis should change.

## Gross versus net

Revenue less direct data, infrastructure, processing and customer support is **gross profit**. Subtracting fixed payroll, sales, product development, insurance, legal and other operating expenses is needed before discussing operating profitability. Taxes, financing and investor returns are separate again.
