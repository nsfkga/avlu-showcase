# Agricultural service readiness | The separate R&D direction

**Historical product research v1.0.0 | October 14, 2026 | Nathaniel Schroeder**

## Why I explored this

My September 2026 equipment-dealer discovery work surfaced a different operational question from buying inventory: **can a dealer catch missing field-service requirements before an avoidable return trip?** That work informed a planned workflow called **AvLü Equipment: Service Readiness and Recovery**.

This was not an arbitrary agriculture pivot. It arose from investigating how established equipment organizations use multiple systems, internal experience, parts availability, equipment configuration and coordination when serving farm operators.

## Proposed service-record flow

1. Record exact machine and implement identity, hours, recent changes and customer-confirmed symptoms.
2. Distinguish information extracted by software from authoritative fitment, technician approval and physical checks.
3. Keep candidate parts, stock on hand, reserved stock and loaded job-kit items as different states.
4. Track specialist tools, technician needs, branch logistics, coverage approvals and customer work-window constraints.
5. Show unresolved preparation dependencies, owners and deadlines without silently overriding the service manager's proceed/defer authority.
6. Capture the pre-visit suspicion, actual findings, work performed and whether recovery was independently confirmed.
7. Analyze repeated preparation failures only once enough reliable outcome records exist.

## Research and test evidence

The historical requirements define branch/scope permissions, source provenance, readiness dependencies, qualified fitment, confirmed outcomes and separate manager authority. A fictional reference suite for ten requirements recorded **10/10** passing checks; a separate dealer-rules reference suite recorded **8/8** synthetic checks.

**These are design/consistency tests, not deployed dealer-service capabilities.** The private runtime review did not establish production job-kit, parts-reservation, cross-branch logistics or dealer-tenant service workflows. No measured first-time-fix improvement, authenticated real dealer pilot, or existing customer adoption is claimed.

## How it relates to the current product

The current approved primary direction is **autonomous acquisition underwriting** with equipment-first evidence, market history and cautious operating boundaries. The service-readiness proposal remains useful **adjacent R&D** because it explores the same asset-identity, configuration, source verification and approval problems at a different stage of the equipment lifecycle.

It should not be merged into the acquisition product in a way that doubles the development scope or implies a service module is already live.

## Research questions worth testing later

- How frequently do missing job requirements cause avoidable repeat visits?
- Do the dealer's current tools already flag those gaps?
- Can a preparation checklist reduce time or costs without adding cumbersome manual entry?
- Who approves action, owns the data and can legally share records for evaluation?
- Would a partner integration be a better route than independent dealer-service software?
