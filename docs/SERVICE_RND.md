# Related research | Equipment Service Readiness and Recovery

**v2.0.0 | Historical R&D only | Nathaniel Schroeder**

Alongside acquisition screening, I explored a separate equipment-dealer problem: incomplete intake, mismatched machine/implement information, parts/tool uncertainty and service coordination before a field visit.

The proposed workflow would track:

- Actual machine, configuration and attachment or implement identity.
- Confirmed versus proposed service approach.
- Parts fitment and availability as distinct states.
- Technician resources, owners, open questions and deadlines.
- Dealer-approved dispatch decisions and actual job outcome.
- Reasons for repeat visits or incomplete service.

**What exists:** A written research roadmap, detailed requirements for a controlled readiness record, and small fictional reference acceptance checks. A separate synthetic dealer-rules reference suite recorded 8/8 passes.

**What does not exist as a validated commercial product:** Real dealer integration, production parts/dispatch synchronization, proven first-visit improvements, or a funded customer pilot for this service-readiness workflow.

The current primary roadmap is **autonomous acquisition underwriting with evidence and conservative controls**. Service readiness remains an adjacent research branch, not the publicly offered production product.

I am preserving this research because it provides useful insight into equipment identity, fitment, parts availability and workflow handoffs, but it should not be mistaken for an implemented acquisition feature.
