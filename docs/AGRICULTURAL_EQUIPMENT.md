# Agricultural equipment | The actual product scope

**Public research brief v1.0.0 | October 14, 2026 | Nathaniel Schroeder**

## Why agriculture matters to AvLü

Agricultural and compact equipment are not a new marketing label applied to an automotive product. My work on **auction-sourced inventory, equipment attachments, practical resale economics and inventory tracking** predates the current software. It exposed a repeatable problem: apparent resale spread can disappear once identity, attachments, configuration, transaction costs and resale timing are considered.

The original work involved multiple kinds of small commercial and farm-adjacent inventory, including **root rakes, pallet forks, buckets, fixed blades, snow pushers, flail-mowing equipment, pressure washers and loader-related attachments**. I continue to research powered machines, but I am not pretending all equipment categories are interchangeable.

**Current product direction:** evidence-led acquisition underwriting, with an initially limited **non-powered compact-equipment attachment** policy. Automotive research remains supported. The separately designed agricultural service-readiness concept is documented as an earlier R&D track, not a validated deployed service product.

## Three rings of scope

| Ring | Assets and questions | Current treatment |

A category title is **not** a mechanical configuration. A root rake can incorporate hydraulic grapple components. A product labeled "snow pusher" at auction can actually be a powered dozer blade. A generic catalog description cannot be treated as proof of safe inclusion.

## Fields a serious equipment screen needs

**Exact identity:** manufacturer, model, serial or other attributable identifier, production year where known, description, source and sale lot. For powered equipment, record hours and machine class. For attachments, record width, mount, hydraulic/auxiliary requirements, tine/edge configuration, weight and capacity, always with evidence state.

**Condition:** photographs, wear points, damage, weld repairs, cracks, moving parts, tires or tracks where relevant, known service history, missing components, stated versus verified condition.

**Fitment:** loader and attachment interface, host-machine compatibility, hydraulic requirement, electrical controls, capacity and geometry. A seller's "fits skid steer" claim is not a verified fitment record.

**Market context:** like-for-like sold results where available, nearby asking inventory, date/region, seasonality, demand, dealer channel, quality differences and practical buyer pool.

**Economics:** actual hammer or asking price, premiums and taxes as applicable, freight and handling, loading, reconditioning, holding, expected selling expense and realistic time to resale.

**Proof gates:** seller authority, ownership/liens, individual unit photos, inspection, transportation, repair evidence and final cost. Incomplete material evidence must return an unresolved review state.

## Seasonal and location considerations

Agricultural demand is not a single national average. A harvest-timed combine, a winter snow pusher and a pallet-fork assembly have different holding windows and buyer populations. Transporting a compact attachment between Minnesota and North Dakota has different cost and access issues from moving a wheel loader across multiple states.

My initial research geography is centered on **Fargo and the surrounding North Dakota/Minnesota market**, with wider Midwest comparisons when appropriate. A distant sold result can be a market signal, but geographic mismatch, freight and lot-specific premiums must be explicit.

## What real research has already taught me

My archived early-2026 mixed-equipment inventory tracked **71 acquisition line items representing 79 units**. It recorded first-party purchase economics and projected retail scenarios. The retained sales log was empty when summarized, so the projected spread is **not evidence of realized profits**. This is a concrete operating-history input, not a generic fictitious sample.

A later historical attachment trace failed to recover the original exact lot, unit configuration and unit-level invoice reconciliation. The unresolved unit remains **HOLD**. There is also a manufacturer's-versus-retailer's published weight discrepancy for a related root-rake family, illustrating why transport weight should not be guessed.

Separate public auction research retained **18 reported pallet-fork sale outcomes**, but source clustering, weak exact-lot attribution for many records and incomplete match criteria prevent treating the sample median as a supported category valuation. Details are in [Auction Case Studies](AG_AUCTION_CASE_STUDIES.md).
