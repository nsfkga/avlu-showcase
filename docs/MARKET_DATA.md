# Market observations, provenance and realized outcomes

**v2.3.0 | October 15, 2026 | Nathaniel Schroeder**

A central AvLü direction is to let a buyer monitor inventory opportunities over time and see supported resale potential **without confusing an advertised price with a completed profitable sale**.

## Why Facebook Marketplace belongs in the source map

Facebook Marketplace is part of the **vehicle and equipment listing opportunity pipeline**, alongside auctions, dealer listings and private-sale websites. The existing private source selector recognizes it for both categories. Earlier Marketplace candidate/QA research exists, but those rows should not be treated as a legally licensed live feed.

A Marketplace asking price remains **a seller ask**. A removed or "sold" listing without attributable settlement documentation cannot be promoted into completed-sale proceeds. Search links and user-provided screenshot/description observations carry timestamps, usage-rights and missing-proof flags. Seller identities or screenshots should not be republished without permission.

This matters when comparing an inexpensive Marketplace skid steer bucket with an auction lot or retail dealer asking price: attachment mount, condition, location, age of observation and transport may make them incomparable. [Marketplace strategy and legal boundaries](FACEBOOK_MARKETPLACE_STRATEGY.md).

## Distinct evidence types

| Type | Meaning | Example implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listing asking price | Seller's requested amount at an observed time | A signal, not a sale |
| Current auction bid | Price shown before a completed settlement | Neither the final hammer nor cost basis |
| Hammer / auction result | Closing outcome if independently confirmed | Must still include premium, tax, services and attribution |
| Comparable advertised listing | Current market supply asking level | Does not establish a sold price |
| Comparable completed-sale result | Recorded sale of a sufficiently related unit | Can help only when match, date and costs are understood |
| Acquisition cost | What the buyer actually incurred | Requires purchase and attributable fee evidence |
| Projected resale | Scenario estimate | Not revenue earned |
| Realized proceeds | Verified received amount from the specific actual sale | One necessary ingredient for realized profit |
| Realized margin | Completed transaction result after attributable costs | Requires matched acquisition, disposal and cost detail |

## Data lifecycle being developed

**Observe → attribute source and timestamp → link exact asset identity/configuration → check freshness/conflicts → classify permitted use → build a conservative comp set → estimate complete cost exposure → record decision → link verified outcome.**

A good result cannot simply be the highest resale price on the internet. Matches need appropriate year, asset type, condition, location, options, hours/mileage, fitment and timing. Unknowns should remain unknown.

## Evidence tiers

**Tier A, complete sale economics:** exact unit, documented purchase, full attributable costs, verified sale proceeds and holding period.

**Tier B, attributable source or market outcome:** exact listing or sale with credible identity/source, but missing part of the purchase-to-resale link.

**Tier C, market research signal:** asking prices, partial public ads, generic product-family pages, incomplete listing evidence or synthetic samples.

These tiers are a conceptual description for reviewers; they are not a disclosure of the private scoring algorithm.

## Current outcome limitations

- There are public ended-auction observations without authoritative final settlement economics.
- A small public sold-result set exists for a non-powered attachment category, but the sample is heterogeneous, clustered and **not matched ask-to-sale pairs**. That category remains uncalibrated for live buying.
- Historical acquisition-cost evidence exists in private records, but matched completed resale proceeds and all realized costs remain unresolved.
- Public vehicle and equipment QA includes many candidate records and comparables, but strict matching rejects most weak relationships and can leave a full three-comp set unavailable.
- The target of broad automatic collection and always-current comparable outcomes **has not yet been verified as an active live service**.

## What I would measure

For each source and category, assess observed coverage, permission and source stability, completeness, cross-source identity matches, false-positive relationships, staleness, corrected prices, matched outcomes, actual fees and seller/inspection proof burden.

For each historical decision, compare the information known *at the decision date* with the eventual verified outcome. A replay that can peek at the final outcome is not evidence that the original recommendation was good.

I intend to use real results to calibrate category-specific uncertainty and decline to issue supported bid limits where the outcome data cannot justify them.
